Understanding Averages

What is an average?

An average (also called arithmetic mean) is a single number that represents the central or typical value of a set of numbers. It's calculated by adding all numbers together and dividing by how many numbers you have.

Example: The average of 10, 20, and 30 is (10 + 20 + 30) ÷ 3 = 20

How do you calculate an average?

Follow these simple steps:

  1. Add all the numbers together to get the sum
  2. Count how many numbers you have
  3. Divide the sum by the count

Formula: Average = Sum ÷ Count

What is the arithmetic mean?

The arithmetic mean is the formal mathematical name for what most people call the "average." It's one of the most common measures of central tendency used in statistics.

This is the exact type of calculation our calculator performs.

What is the difference between average and median?

The average (mean) and median are different measures:

  • Average (Mean): The sum of all numbers divided by the count
  • Median: The middle value when numbers are arranged in order

Example: For numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 100:

  • Average: (1+2+3+4+100) ÷ 5 = 22
  • Median: 3 (the middle number)

The median is often better when you have outliers (extremely large or small numbers).

What is the difference between average and mode?

The average and mode are different statistical measures:

  • Average (Mean): Sum of all numbers divided by the count
  • Mode: The number that appears most frequently

Example: For numbers 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4:

  • Average: (1+2+2+2+3+4) ÷ 6 = 2.33
  • Mode: 2 (appears three times)
